1. What is the fundamental objective of this project? ▼
To build an explainable supervised machine learning system that classifies students into "Good", "Average", or "At Risk" categories early in the semester based on attendance, marks, assignments, previous scores, and study habits, facilitating proactive academic counseling.
2. Why is this formulated as a Classification problem rather than Regression? ▼
Regression predicts continuous numbers (e.g. 78.4% mark), whereas classification maps inputs into discrete qualitative tiers ('Good', 'Average', 'At Risk') which are directly actionable for institutional intervention.
3. Why did you choose Random Forest over a single Decision Tree? ▼
Single decision trees have high variance and tend to overfit noisy student data. Random Forest builds an ensemble of 100 decorrelated decision trees using bagging (bootstrap aggregation) and takes a majority vote, significantly reducing variance and boosting generalizability.
4. Why does Random Forest NOT require feature scaling (normalization)? ▼
Tree-based algorithms partition data using rank-order threshold inequalities (e.g., Attendance ≤ 75%). Scaling transformations preserve rank ordering, meaning scale differences (e.g. 0-100% vs 0-12 hours) do not distort tree split choices.
5. What is the purpose of Stratified Sampling in Train-Test Split? ▼
Stratified sampling ensures both the training set (80%) and the testing set (20%) contain the exact same proportion of 'Good', 'Average', and 'At Risk' classes as the overall population, preventing sampling bias.
6. How does the model calculate prediction probabilities? ▼
In Random Forest, class probability is the percentage of trees in the ensemble that voted for that specific class. For example, if 88 of 100 trees vote "Good", the confidence is 88.0%.
7. Does the application use hardcoded IF-ELSE rules for prediction? ▼
No. The prediction and probabilities are produced entirely by the trained Random Forest model loaded via Joblib. IF-ELSE rules are only used to generate auxiliary advisory tips (e.g. alerting if attendance < 75%).
8. What is the difference between Precision and Recall for the 'At Risk' class? ▼
Precision is the fraction of predicted 'At Risk' students who were genuinely at risk (avoids unnecessary panic). Recall is the fraction of all actual 'At Risk' students that the model successfully caught (avoids students failing without warning).
